Process should make
delivery lighter.

How lightweight definitions, checklists, and visible risks can give teams more focus instead of more bureaucracy.

Sample articleProduct delivery
On this pageThe symptomsThe minimum systemMeasure the effect

Process is easy to add and difficult to remove. A team can accumulate meetings, templates, and approval steps until the work feels organised while becoming slower.

Notice the symptoms

When work repeatedly arrives without enough context, the same questions appear late. Risks surface during release week. Stakeholders discover surprises in sprint reviews. These are signals that the system needs more clarity, not necessarily more ceremony.

Build the minimum useful system

A lightweight delivery rhythm can start with a few shared expectations:

  1. A Definition of Ready for work entering a sprint.
  2. A Definition of Done that includes quality and release needs.
  3. A short sprint-health checklist for risks and dependencies.
  4. A visible place for decisions, assumptions, and open issues.
The point of process is to protect attention for the work that needs judgement.

Measure the effect

Good process should show up in outcomes: fewer avoidable interruptions, clearer ownership, more predictable releases, and faster recovery when something does go wrong.

Keep adapting

Teams should be able to change the playbook when it stops helping. The best delivery standards are living agreements, not paperwork that outlives its purpose.
