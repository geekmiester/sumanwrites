Context

As part of an internal platform initiative, I reviewed how users moved from first login to their first meaningful action. The product was technically functional, but many steps were hidden, unclear, or inconsistent with how users actually thought about setup.

Where the friction lived

  • Important setup actions were hidden behind unclear navigation paths.
  • Users had to infer what to do before they saw any value from the product.
  • Support content existed, but it was disconnected from the live workflow.

What I changed

  • Mapped the full first-run journey to identify where users stalled or disengaged.
  • Introduced a simplified activation checklist with clear status markers and reduced cognitive load.
  • Added contextual guidance at the exact moments users needed support.

Outcome

  • Users reached first value faster because the product gave them a clearer, more intentional path.
  • Support needs reduced because onboarding content was easier to understand and navigate.
  • The product team had a repeatable framework for testing future activation improvements.

At a glance

  • Role: Product Owner
  • Context: Internal onboarding workflow
  • Impact: 30% less drop-off

Core lesson

Onboarding works best when it gives users a small, confident next step rather than a long list of tasks.
