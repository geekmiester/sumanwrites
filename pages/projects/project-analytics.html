Context

Product teams had enough data to be busy, but not enough clarity to act confidently. Metrics were fragmented across tools and did not connect directly to the questions that mattered most for product decisions.

What I found

  • Dashboards existed, but they were only partial views of the actual product story.
  • Signals were not tied to product outcomes or roadmap decisions.
  • Reporting was useful for reporting, not for making decisions.

What I changed

  • Defined a small set of product metrics tied to user outcomes and business intent.
  • Created a reporting rhythm that connected customer feedback, product usage, and roadmap trade-offs.
  • Set a consistent review cadence so product teams could respond earlier and with more context.

Decision loop model

Outcome

  • Product progress became easier to explain to stakeholders and leaders.
  • Teams started making decisions with a clearer evidence trail.
  • Product discussions moved from anecdotes toward better, shared interpretation of signals.

At a glance

  • Role: Product Manager / Product Owner
  • Context: Product health and adoption data
  • Impact: Faster product decisions

Key principle

Metrics should answer product questions, not simply decorate a dashboard. The real value is in what those signals help us do next.
