About / The longer version

Why the work
matters to me.

I’m interested in the space between a real problem, a team’s ability to solve it, and what changes when the product reaches people.

From code to context

I started by building software. I stayed for the questions behind it.

I began my career as a Mobile Application Developer. Over time, I found myself increasingly drawn to the decisions around the code: why a feature should exist, which problem deserved attention, how teams prioritised, and how users experienced what we shipped.

Moving into product management gave me a wider view of that work. I could bring client conversations, delivery constraints, design, engineering, and commercial context into the same conversation without pretending they always point in the same direction.

I still tend to think about product decisions with one eye on how they will actually get built. That technical foundation helps me ask better questions and work constructively with specialists, while keeping the user and the intended outcome in view.

Working with uncertainty

Turning ambiguity into something a team can build.

I start with the problem. I try to understand stakeholders, talk to users, look at the evidence that exists, and separate assumptions from facts. Then I define an outcome and break a large question into testable increments.

The goal is not to pretend uncertainty has disappeared. It is to give a team enough clarity to move, while keeping the important unknowns visible.

A practical balance

Speed, scope, and quality are always in the room.

Product management often means making imperfect decisions with incomplete information. Delivery deadlines, user value, engineering complexity, commercial priorities, quality, and technical risk all matter at once.

I try to make those trade-offs explicit, bring the right people into the decision, and keep the team focused on the outcome rather than the appearance of certainty.
