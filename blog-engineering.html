Working with engineers
when the answer is unclear.

Questions, constraints, and shared context are often more useful than arriving with false certainty.

Sample articleTechnical product management

Product and engineering do not need to agree immediately for collaboration to work. They do need a shared understanding of the problem, the constraints, and the decision that has to be made.

Share the context before the answer

Engineers make better trade-offs when they understand the user situation, business reason, expected outcome, and time sensitivity. A requirements list without context leaves too much of the product decision hidden.

Ask the questions together

Useful questions are often simple:

  • What is the smallest version that changes the user situation?
  • What technical constraint should shape the product choice?
  • Which risk is acceptable now, and which one needs to be reduced?
  • What can we learn after release?
Technical constraints are product context. Product outcomes are engineering context.

Make the call and keep learning

Eventually a team needs a decision. The goal is not consensus on every detail. It is clarity about what we are doing, why, what we are trading away, and when we will look again.

Respect specialist judgement

Good product leadership makes the problem and priorities clear, then gives specialists room to solve within those boundaries. That is how technical quality and user value become part of the same conversation.
